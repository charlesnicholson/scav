Phases 2 and 3

Decorated states: headers, bands and loops

What an application draws inside a state is reserved before layout runs, and layout keeps every route, port and label out of it.

Headers and bands

The reference builder draws a PlantUML-style header at the top of each state: the name, centred; then, on a state with a description or a live region, a rule from border to border with the description under it. It reserves exactly the header's height.

A band is interior room a state's BoxSpace reserves around its submachine area: h_before above, h_after below, and w_before and w_after before and after it, between the other two. Layout returns each as a rect (state_before, state_after, state_lead, state_trail) for the application to draw in, and the box formula adds all four. A header is an h_before band.

RuleEvery band is an obstacle, because an application reserves room only for what it draws there. No route enters a band, no label overlaps one, and no port sits on a face a band lines. As walls, the side bands run from the top band's top to the bottom band's bottom, so no seam opens where two bands meet. A route may still end on a lined face: an arrow to the state itself touches its border and enters nothing.

A state's bands and loop room are walls in every frame inside it that they overlap, so a route inside a headed composite goes round the header as it goes round a child, and nudging keeps lanes out of them. A route that enters one anyway is counted by Tier 0's through_band.

Ports keep off lined faces

A face is lined when a band covers it: the top by h_before, the bottom by h_after, the left by w_before and the right by w_after. A route crossing a composite's border does so at a port, and from a port on a lined face it would have to cross the band to reach the child. Since the reference builder heads every state, a route never enters a composite through its top.

This holds by construction, not by price:

  • The facing pass never turns a port onto a lined face, and moves a port off one: to the opposite border if that is not lined, else to a top or bottom border that the child it joins can see past its siblings.
  • The search never offers a lined face as a side move.
  • If none of those is free, the request cannot be met. The port keeps its face, its route crosses the band, through_band counts the crossing, and layout still succeeds.

A net the walls seal off is walled. The router routes it once more on a grid that blocks boxes only, so it crosses the band with an axis-aligned leg; RouteWalled traces it, through_band counts it, and the net is not degraded. Since a crossed band is Tier 0, the search's side moves take any face still free, and a port ships a crossing only where every face of its state is lined. An inner loop never crosses one.

Loops inside a state

An internal or local self-transition is a loop inside its source. A state's loops share its loop room, which phase 2 reserves in its free interior, the interior inside the pad ring less the four bands. The room takes one of eight placements: a face of the free interior, at that face's leading or trailing end. Unpinned, it takes the right face's trailing end, or the left face's where a band lines the right face and none lines the left. A LoopPin sets the placement, and the search tries the other seven.

Each loop leaves the room's face from inside, runs in to its far leg, and returns beside where it left with its arrowhead on the same face. Its legs end on that face's boundary: the border where that side has no band, and the band's inner edge where one lines it. A top-face loop on a headed state therefore leaves the header's bottom edge, and no loop crosses a band. On a left or right face the loops are rows with horizontal legs; on a top or bottom face the room turns, and the loops stand side by side with vertical legs. A loop's lane is at least as tall as its label stack, with a route clearance either side.

The room is sized from the loops' PathBoxes alone, so phase 3 and the cost re-derive it and no geometry column carries it. Phase 3 draws each loop in its row instead of routing it. The room is a wall for every other route and runs on to the boundary its loops reach, so a route inside the state goes round it and never across a leg. The labels are not searched: they sit beyond the far leg, at most a leader from it, so every loop label is within a leader of its route.

Where a loop's ends touch its state's border, the run between them, padded by a route clearance, is an occupied span of that face. The router seats no outside end in it, and a port slot or inner-face end on it moves along the face to the nearest free position. With no free position the end stays and Routes::occupied counts it.

Loops outside a state

An external self-transition is a net whose two ends name its state. The attach pass seats both ends on the face of the state that the fewest other ends use, the right face on a tie; the loop pass then puts them a line of text apart about the middle of the widest run of that face that no other seat, port slot or inner-face end stands in and no occupied span covers. The route runs out 2·pad from each seat and back. An end pin may move either end, so the search can take a loop round a corner.

Every transition is internal to its lowest common ancestor

A route never crosses the border of the state that holds both its ends: it is drawn inside that state, through a port on each state between. A transition's kind only sets the default. Between two states of one machine a transition is external to each of them; one leaving a machine is internal to the composite holding both ends. An internal self-transition is the case where that composite is the state itself, so it is drawn inside it like any other.

An internal transition from a composite into its own child starts on the composite's border, level with the boundary node ordering placed for it, unless another region of the composite lies between.
