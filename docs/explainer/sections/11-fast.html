Speed without changing a drawing

Making the search fast, exactly

Every optimisation here is exact: with or without it, every drawing is byte-identical.

RuleEach optimisation has a test mode that runs the unoptimised computation alongside and compares, with a planted defect proving the comparison can fail.

Every optimisation exploits one fact: a candidate is the incumbent plus one pin, so almost everything it computes has been computed before. Before them, one bottler layout ran Brandes–Köpf 19.8 million times for 69,496 distinct graphs. A general lower bound does not help, because most losing candidates land within 1% of the incumbent; the label bound below works because it bounds only the label half of the cost.

Frame memos

Each thread keeps three memos, each keyed on everything its step reads: Brandes–Köpf by the whole graph, and each frame's sizing and ordering by that frame's inputs and pins. Keys are interned, so a frame is named by a serial number rather than spelled out. Each memo is capped at 4 MiB per thread, and traced runs bypass them.

Ordering and sizing reuse

Even a memo hit builds and hashes a key; reuse skips that. An ordering records what each frame read beyond the graph (its pins, label charges, orientation and the profile). A candidate compares those records with the incumbent's, orders only the frames that differ, and copies the rest. Sizing does the same, except that a frame's size depends on its children's, so every frame holding a changed one is re-sized too. Most frames are copied.

The face shortcut

Ordering, sizing and the facing pass never read a face pin, so a face candidate starts from the incumbent's sizing and only routes and scores, at about a quarter of the cost; it also keeps every label nothing it reads has changed. A face that would not move its seat draws the incumbent exactly, so it is charged but not scored, and faces are offered only where the move table says they are priced.

The label bound

Labels are placed last and read only the routes, so a round with labels runs in two passes:

  1. Route every candidate without labels and score a lower bound: label terms are zero, and area is measured on the unlabelled chart.
  2. Visit candidates whose bound beats the incumbent, least bound first. Label and score each exactly while its bound can still beat the best so far; stop at the first that cannot.

The pick is exactly what scoring every candidate whole would give, and most candidates never place their labels. The 128 most promising candidates keep their routes from the first pass, so their exact pass does not route again.

Smaller wins

  • No allocation: every stage fills per-thread buffers reused across candidates; ordering keeps one per nesting depth, because a waiting thread may run a nested candidate.
  • Radix A* queue: A* pops in a strict total order, so any exact priority queue gives the same sequence; a radix heap is faster.
  • Integer square root by Newton's method from above, checked exhaustively over all 32-bit inputs.
  • One global pool: nested work (rows, searches, candidate rounds) shares one pool, so no call site divides the cores among its children, and the search memo removes repeated searches.
