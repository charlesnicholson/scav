What scav optimises

The objective: three tiers, compared in order

Every candidate drawing gets one Cost, and the search keeps the least, so the cost defines what "better" means.

A cost is a triple (t0, t1, t2) compared lexicographically: lower t0 wins outright, then t1, then t2. There are no multipliers between tiers, because a huge weight inside one sum is a cliff that breaks local search and can overflow. Cost reads only the finished geometry. Touching never counts as overlapping, and segments cross only properly, not at a shared endpoint.

Tier 0: forbidden, not priced

t0 counts things a reader must never see; one outweighs any Tier 2 total. Most are impossible by construction, so Tier 0 is a safety net for degraded routes, trial drawings and label fallbacks. Every shipped example has t0 = 0.

Tier 1: hints

t1 is reserved for author's layout hints, which are not built; it is always 0.

Tier 2: thirteen weighted terms

t2 is an integer sum. Each term is converted to a unit first: counts stay counts, lengths are divided by the em (the font size, 192 units on readable) and areas by em², rounding up. Then it is multiplied by its weight.

Weights are capped at 1,024, which keeps the sum inside 64 bits.

Weights are exchange rates

With every term in its own unit, a weight says how much of one defect a reader would trade for another. On readable, one bend costs the same as one crossing, one em of crowded lanes, 64 em of route length, or 512 em² of canvas. Without the unit conversion, area (degree 2 in the coordinates) swamped every other term. The weights were then fitted to pick the drawing with the fewest defects a reader counts.

Shares

Each term's share of t2, in basis points, is committed per chart beside the cost goldens, so a reviewer sees what a weight change bought and sold.
