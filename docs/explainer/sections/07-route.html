Phase 3

Routing: ports, faces, A*, seats and lanes

Phase 3 draws every segment as an axis-aligned polyline; states are obstacles, so a route through a box cannot be represented at all.

Each segment becomes a net. Every net's ends are seated, then frames are routed in parallel (a frame that has not changed reuses its previous answer), nets are joined into transitions, ends are trimmed for arrowheads, parallel lanes are nudged apart, and labels are placed last.

Ports and the facing pass

A port slot is where a route crosses a composite's border. In a frame running left to right, ports on the left are sources and on the right sinks; ports may also sit on the top or bottom. Sides are numbered 0 left, 1 right, 2 top, 3 bottom.

After the first sizing, the facing pass turns ports toward their route's far end, then reruns ordering and sizing if any turned:

  • a port whose far end lies on the frame's other side has its edge reversed, moving it to the opposite border;
  • a port on its own transition's state whose far end lies further away across the ranks than along them moves to the top or bottom border facing it, if that border is unobstructed and free. A side pin is never turned.

Which face an end leaves by

The separation rule seats each end on a face of its box: the axis on which the far end lies further outside the box wins (ties to the layering axis), and the nearer border on that axis is the face. The seat is the far end's projection onto the face, held off the corners. Inscribed glyphs (discs, diamonds) take the face's midpoint.

A face pin overrides the rule at one end, unless the face is too short to hold a seat. Choosing both faces as a pair was tried and made most charts worse, so the face is a search dimension, not a rule.

The orthogonal router

The router runs A* on a separated orthogonal visibility graph:

  • Grid: lines through every obstacle edge grown by the clearance, every wall and every route end; an edge is passable unless it enters a grown obstacle. A frame over 218 vertices is refused.
  • Clearance is a bumper, not a penalty: no route comes within clear = node_sep / 3 (96) of a box, by construction.
  • Two planes: each vertex has a horizontal and a vertical copy, joined by an edge costing the bend penalty (one rank_sep), so plain A* minimises length and bends together. A turn at a fixed end costs slightly less, so equal paths turn at their ends.
  • Deterministic: the heuristic is admissible, and the open list is totally ordered by (f, g, node), so the result never depends on the queue. The search is capped at 220 expansions.
  • Ring points: an end is pushed out by the clearance, and the leg back to the box is emitted, not searched, so it is perpendicular for free.

A chained segment's bend nodes are waypoints the route must pass, leg by leg. Each frame's obstacles are its live states except the ones the route lives inside; walls keep a route inside the state it is drawn in.

Seating passes

Every seat is chosen before any net is searched, because two ends wanting one seat is a question about the pair. Five passes run in order:

  1. Attach: seat each end by the separation rule or its face pin.
  2. Reface (glyphs): when an arrival and a departure want one midpoint, move the one with more to gain to another face.
  3. Align: put both ends of a net between parallel faces on one coordinate, so one straight segment fits; a net with label room reserved takes the low end, and corridor nets are skipped.
  4. Spread: split ends sharing a point by direction of travel, a line of text apart where the face has room, else closer; repeat until nothing moves.
  5. Separate: push apart legs on different boxes that run closer than a line of text (fans exempt).

Nudging lanes apart

Nudging spreads routes that run alongside each other, per frame and again over the whole chart.

  • A lane is a group of overlapping parallel segments closer than one em (192).
  • Within a lane, segments are ordered by where they came from, and reordered where that avoids a crossing; fan-ins and fan-outs move as one bundle.
  • Segments spread up to one em apart, within the room their frame and obstacles leave.
  • A move is kept only if it creates no collapsed leg, no new overlap with a box or its bumper, no new run along a border and no new shared run; otherwise the bundle stays put. A drawing that overlaps a box is worse than any score.

Trims and failures

PathClear shortens each route's first and last legs for arrowheads, by at most half the leg. A net fails if an end lies outside its region, if the frame's grid is too large, or if it is unreachable even with zero clearance. A failed net is drawn as a straight line with a RouteDegraded diagnostic.

Only unreachable nets trigger the inflation retry, since the others do not depend on spacing: rank_sep, node_sep and sub_sep grow by 32 units on a copy of the profile and sizing and routing rerun, up to 8 times, keeping the least-degraded attempt. The cost is still scored under the caller's profile, and the search never accepts an inflated candidate: fitting only after widening everything is a bigger drawing, not a better one.
